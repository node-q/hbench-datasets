## What this is

A test set on rating one insurer's commercial property program in Ohio. Each of its 75 tasks gives the AI the manual pages the insurer filed and states the facts in the question, such as a building's construction and its amount of insurance. The AI gives exact values: a limit factor read between table rows, a rate or premium after a given step, the charge for an endorsement, a flood premium, or the errors in the filed pages. The tasks fall into fourteen kinds of work, such as flood, schedule rating and Ohio's own rules.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has the insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads real documents from the insurer's public rate filings for Ohio: its countrywide manual exception pages (edition 0426) and its Ohio exception page (edition 0226). Both were confirmed as filed and in effect, with nothing replacing them, on 6 November 2026. A few tasks ask what an earlier edition said. They name the edition, and for them the files also hold the 0226 countrywide pages and the insurer's redlines between editions.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names and codes. The insurer, and nine other companies, brands and associations named in property class names, read [Company A] to [Company J]. The insurer's own form and endorsement numbers are replaced by neutral codes (FORM-001 to FORM-049), and the document number in each page footer by a marker. No filing number, link or key back to the real names is kept. A scan of every file found no personal data.

The pages amend ISO's Commercial Lines Manual. The countrywide pages restate text and tables that follow ISO's manual, such as the interpolation rule, the flood tables and the rating plan's risk characteristics. Those parts may be ISO's copyright.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real business, and no application or submission was made up: the only documents are the insurer's own. Each answer was worked out step by step from the filed pages. Separate scripts then read every rate and table again on their own and recomputed every answer key. Each task grades 2 to 8 fields, 424 in all. Whole-dollar amounts that the filing rounds must match exactly. Other money counts as right within a dollar or 0.1%.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and not the insurer. Where the filing is silent or unclear, the tasks leave the question out, but you may still disagree with how an answer key reads the text.
- It is one insurer's program, rated in Ohio, for small commercial risks. Other states' pages are left out.
- The territory base rates and ISO's relativity tables are not in the filing, so no task rates a full building or business personal property premium from a limit. Where a task needs a rate, the question gives it.
- Rules the filing leaves open are not asked, among them water back-up premiums and the rate to use for a mixed occupancy.
- The documents are text taken from the filed PDFs. Wide tables are harder to read than on the page, and the redlines lose their strike-through and underline.
