## What this is

A test set on rating one insurer's private passenger auto program in Ohio. Each of its 60 tasks gives the AI documents the insurer filed and states the facts in the question, such as a vehicle's model and year, a driver's insurance score or a given tier. The AI gives exact values: a vehicle symbol, a garaging or driver factor, a partial premium in the filed rate order, a discount, a fee, a rule, or the errors in the filing. The tasks fall into 13 kinds of work, such as vehicle symbols, fees and the policy term.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has the insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads real documents from one public rate, rule and form filing for a new auto program in Ohio: the rules, whole pages taken from the rate pages, three vehicle symbol lists with the method behind them, the policy form, the explanatory memorandum and two sheets of a filed tier workbook. Each task gets only the pages it needs. The rules and rate pages were confirmed as filed and in effect, with nothing replacing them, on 6 November 2026.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names, contact details and one form code. The memorandum signer's name, e-mail address and phone number are blanked out. The insurer and every other company named in the documents read [Company A], [Company B] and so on, the score vendors [Vendor A] to [Vendor C], a vendor's score product [Program A], and the filing this program copies [Filing A]. The insurer's address is replaced by a marker and its form code by a neutral code. Vehicle makes and models are kept. No key back to the real names is kept.

The memorandum says the program copies another insurer's approved filing, so the rules and rate relativities may repeat that insurer's material.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real driver or vehicle owner, and no application, driver record or credit report was made up: the only documents are the filing's own. Each answer was read step by step from the filed pages. A separate script then read every rate, factor and rule again from the text and recomputed all 60 answer keys, 256 graded fields in all, and they agreed. Each task grades 2 to 7 fields.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and not the insurer. Where the filing is silent, unclear or contradicts itself, the tasks leave the question out or ask only what each document prints.
- No task works out a full premium. The rate cap has no table in the filing, and the rules and the rate pages disagree on the minimum premium and the policy term. The tasks ask for partial premiums instead.
- No task places a driver in a tier from their facts, because the filed tier workbook needs a value the filing does not give. The questions state the tier.
- It is one program in Ohio, for new policies only. Renewals are left out, and symbol questions use model years 2014 and later.
- The memorandum and the tier workbook name other states in places, so they appear only in tasks that check the documents.
- The documents are text taken from the filed PDFs. Wide tables wrap or are cut off at the page edge.
