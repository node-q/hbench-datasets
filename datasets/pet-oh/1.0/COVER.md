## What this is

A test set on rating pet health insurance in Ohio. One insurer sells it there as two programs, and each of the 48 tasks works from the documents it filed for them. The question states the facts, such as a dog's breed, its age, the ZIP code and the plan chosen. The AI gives exact values: a breed group, an age or area factor, a discount, a full premium, the monthly payment, a policy term, or the errors in the filings. The tasks fall into ten kinds of work, such as breed classification, ways of paying and the two programs side by side.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has the insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads real documents from the insurer's public rate and form filings for Ohio. For the first program, these are its underwriting rules and rate manual, effective 1 May 2026 and filed as rate sheets for new policies only, and its policy form. One task also gets that program's actuarial memorandum, without its exhibits. For the second program, the AI gets its underwriting rules and rate guidelines, effective 2 April 2026. Every filing was confirmed as filed and in effect for the programs and versions used, with nothing replacing it, on 6 November 2026.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names, contact details and one form number. The signers' names, a signature stamp and a credential number are blanked out. Company, program, vendor and product names read [Company A], [Program A], [Vendor A] and so on, and addresses, phone numbers, e-mail addresses and a website are replaced by markers. The insurer's policy form number is replaced by a neutral code, and the files have neutral names. No key back to the real names is kept.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real pet or owner, and no application or claim was made up: the only documents are the insurer's own. Each answer was worked out step by step by hand from the filed pages. A separate script then read every rate, factor and rule again from the text and recomputed all 258 graded fields, and it matched every one. Each task grades 2 to 8 fields. Money counts as right within a dollar or 0.1%, except where a few cents are the point of the question.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and not the insurer. Where the filings are silent or unclear, the tasks leave the question out or settle the reading in the question, but you may still disagree with how an answer key reads the text.
- It is one insurer's two programs, rated in Ohio only, for dogs and cats only.
- The first program's rate sheets rate new policies only, so no task rates a renewal in it.
- The second program's policy form and the memorandum's exhibits are not included, so no task depends on them.
- The text keeps the PDFs' layout, so wide tables are harder to read than on the page.
