## What this is

A test set on rating one insurer's errors and omissions program in Ohio. Each of its 67 tasks gives the AI the rating manuals and policy forms the insurer filed and states the facts in the question, such as an accounting firm's revenue or the date a claim was made. The AI gives exact values: a base premium, a rating factor, a full premium, an extended reporting premium, or whether a claim is covered. The professions are lawyers, accountants, architects and engineers, miscellaneous professionals, real estate professionals and insurance agencies. The tasks fall into fourteen kinds of work, such as cyber, extended reporting and Ohio's own rules.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has the insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads real documents from one public filing for Ohio: the insurer's rating manuals for each profession, its Ohio state exceptions pages, its countrywide cyber endorsement rules, and the policy forms and endorsements a question needs. The filing was confirmed as filed and in effect, with nothing replacing it, on 6 November 2026.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names and codes. The insurer reads [Company A] and a coverage brand in the cyber rules [Program A]. The insurer's own form and endorsement numbers are replaced by neutral codes (FORM-001 to FORM-033), and the document codes in the page footers by a marker. No filing number, link or key back to the real names is kept. A scan of every file found no personal data. None of the documents carries a rating bureau's copyright notice or reproduces a bureau's loss costs.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real firm, and no application, loss run or other submission was made up: the only documents are the insurer's own. Where the filing leaves open something a question needs, such as how to count days, the question states it. Each answer was worked out step by step by hand from the documents. A separate script then read every rate, factor and table again on its own and recomputed every answer. It caught one answer that rested on how one phrase is read, and that field was replaced. Someone else also solved a sample of the tasks from the documents without seeing the answers. Each task grades 2 to 8 fields, 359 in all. Whole-dollar amounts that the filing rounds must match exactly. Other money counts as right within a dollar or 0.1%.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and not the insurer. Where the filing is silent or unclear, the tasks leave the question out or state how to read it, but you may still disagree with how an answer key reads the text.
- It is one insurer's program for small professional firms, rated in Ohio. The insurer's countrywide manual, withdrawn for Ohio, is left out.
- Factors the underwriter would choose, such as risk complexity and disciplinary debits, are given in the question.
- Extended reporting premiums are rated for lawyers only, because the other manuals leave one factor undefined. Their extended reporting factor tables are still asked.
- The documents are text taken from the filed PDFs, so wide tables are harder to read than on the page.
