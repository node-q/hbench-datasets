## What this is

A test set on the federal rules for grants. Each of its 30 tasks states the facts of an unnamed recipient's Federal award, such as a budget, purchases, spending and dates. The AI works out what the Office of Management and Budget's Uniform Guidance says: which costs are allowable, the modified total direct costs and the indirect costs, what counts as cost sharing or program income, the procurement method, whether a single audit is required, and the due dates that follow. The tasks fall into ten kinds of work, such as allowable costs, procurement and major programs.

This is a draft. No grants accountant, auditor or lawyer, and nobody at the Office of Management and Budget, has checked the answers.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads one document: 2 CFR Part 200, Uniform Administrative Requirements, Cost Principles, and Audit Requirements for Federal Awards (the Uniform Guidance), as the eCFR showed it for 12 November 2026. That text carries the amounts of the 2024 revision, and the questions hold the AI to those amounts. It gets subparts A, B, D, E and F, 159 of the part's 180 sections. Subpart C, the appendices and three sections are left out because they name private organizations or a private party; no task needs them. We turned the XML into plain text and changed no words. The eCFR is an editorial compilation of the Code of Federal Regulations, not an official legal edition of it. This test set is not approved, endorsed or sponsored by the Office of Management and Budget or any other Federal body.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the scenarios. They describe no real organization, and the agencies in them are called Agency A, B and C. Where the text can be read more than one way, the question states the reading it uses. Three procurement tasks give the Federal Acquisition Regulation's purchase thresholds, fifteen thousand and three hundred fifty thousand dollars, as facts of the question. We did not check them against that regulation. Each answer was worked out step by step from the facts and the text. A second script, sharing no code with the first, read every amount it used out of the text itself, recomputed every answer and agreed. Both come from the same reading of the text, so they catch slips of arithmetic, not a different reading of a rule.

## What it does not cover

- It is not accounting, audit or legal advice. A practitioner may disagree with a reading a question states.
- Questions that need someone to weigh the facts are not asked: whether a cost is reasonable or allocable, whether an entity is a subrecipient or a contractor, which programs are high-risk or would be picked as major. The questions state such facts or leave them out.
- Indirect cost rate proposals, the rules on pay and fringe benefits, and the pre-award requirements of subpart C are left out.
- Real awards also answer to statutes, agency regulations and their own terms and conditions. The questions set these aside.
- The recipients are nonprofit organizations, local governments and institutions of higher education, in tidy situations a question can state in a paragraph.
- The answers are for the text as it stood on 12 November 2026.
