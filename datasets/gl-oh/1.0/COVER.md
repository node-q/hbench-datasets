## What this is

A test set on rating one insurer's commercial general liability program in Ohio. Each of its 81 tasks gives the AI the manual pages the insurer filed, with its own worked rating examples, and states the facts in the question, such as a contractor's class or the length of a short policy. The AI gives exact values: a minimum premium, the premium for an endorsement, a rating factor, the Ohio rule that takes the place of a countrywide one, or the errors in the filed pages. The tasks fall into nine kinds of work, such as additional coverages, Ohio's own rules and schedule rating.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has the insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads real documents from the insurer's public rate filings for Ohio: its countrywide manual exception pages (version 0426), its Ohio exception page and a page of its worked rating examples. A few tasks ask what an earlier version said. They also get the 0226 countrywide pages and the insurer's redlines between versions, and they name the version they ask about. Every filing was confirmed as filed and in effect, with nothing replacing it, on 6 November 2026.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names and codes. The insurer reads [Company A] and its score vendor [Vendor A]. The insurer's own form numbers are replaced by neutral codes (FORM-001 and on), and the document codes in the page footers by a marker. No filing number, link or key back to the real names is kept. A scan of every file found no personal data.

The pages amend ISO's Commercial Lines Manual, and the countrywide pages quote some of ISO's class descriptions and territory definitions. Those parts may be ISO's copyright.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real business, and no application or submission was made up: the only documents are the insurer's own. Each answer was worked out step by step by hand from the filed pages. A separate script then read the rates and rules again on its own and recomputed every answer. It caught one wrong hand-worked answer. Each task grades 2 to 8 fields, 465 in all. Whole-dollar amounts that the filing rounds must match exactly. Other money counts as right within $1 or 0.1%.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and not the insurer. Where the filing is silent or unclear, the tasks leave the question out, but you may still disagree with how an answer key reads the text.
- It is one insurer's program, rated in Ohio, for small commercial risks. Other states' pages are left out.
- ISO's loss costs and increased limit factors are not in the filing, so no task rates a full premium from exposure. Where a task needs the premium at an earlier step, the question gives it.
- Rules that leave the value to the underwriter, or give no single answer, are not asked. That includes values between the rows of a table with no rule for reading between them.
- The documents are text taken from the filed PDFs. Wide tables are harder to read than on the page, and the redlines don't mark which text was struck or inserted.
