## What this is

A test set on rating medical professional liability for physicians and surgeons in Ohio, with two insurers' filed programs. Each of its 50 tasks gives the AI both insurers' manuals and states the facts in the question, such as a doctor's specialty, county, limits and claims record. The AI gives exact values: a class and its rate, a territory, a limits factor, a claims-made or tail premium, a credit held to its cap, a full premium, or the errors in the filings. Some tasks rate the same doctor at both insurers. The tasks fall into eleven kinds of work, such as tail coverage, schedule rating and full premiums.

This is a draft. No underwriter has checked the answers, and neither has either insurer.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads six documents from two public rate filings for Ohio. The first insurer's are its Ohio Manual of Rates and Rules, effective 1 August 2026, and its filing exhibits. The second insurer's are its countrywide general rules, its Ohio exception pages, its Ohio rate pages and its 2025 summary of schedule rating, filed together as one manual. Where the countrywide and Ohio pages differ, the tasks follow the Ohio pages, as the countrywide rules direct. Each filing was confirmed as filed and in effect, with nothing replacing it, on 6 November 2026.

We turned the filed PDFs into text and changed nothing but names and codes. The two insurers read [Company A] and [Company B]. The other companies the exhibits cite, two medical associations, a purchasing group, two branded coverages and twelve other insurers' filing numbers get the same kind of stand-in name, such as [Company C], [Organization A], [Program A] and [Filing A]. The first insurer's two form codes are replaced by neutral codes. No key back to the real names is kept. A search of every page found no personal data.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the questions. They describe no real doctor, and no application or submission was made up: the only documents are the insurers' own. Each answer was worked out step by step by hand from the filed pages. A separate script then read every rate, factor and rule from the text on its own and recomputed every answer, and all 239 graded fields matched. A second check found every quote an answer rests on on the page it cites. Each task grades 2 to 8 fields. Money counts as right within a dollar or 0.1%.

## What it does not cover

- Nobody in the trade has reviewed it: no underwriter, and neither insurer. Where the filings are silent or unclear, the tasks leave the question out, but you may still disagree with how an answer key reads the text.
- It covers two insurers' programs for physicians and surgeons, in one state, Ohio.
- The second insurer's manual doesn't say how its discounts combine, so no task applies more than one of them.
- The second insurer's experience rating and the first insurer's rate indication rest on data the filings don't hold, so no task computes them.
- The text keeps the PDFs' layout. Wrapped specialty names and tables with many columns are harder to read than on the page. The stand-in names are longer than the names they replace, so some table headings no longer line up with their columns.
