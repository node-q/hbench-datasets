## What this is

A test set on workplace injury records under US federal law. Each of its 29 tasks describes what happened at an unnamed workplace, and the AI keeps the records the way a safety manager would. It tells first aid from medical treatment by the regulation's closed list, applies the exceptions to work-relatedness, puts each injury or illness in the right column of the log, counts days away and restricted days up to the cap, runs the hearing loss tests, and meets the deadlines for recording, posting, reporting and submitting. The tasks fall into ten kinds of work.

This is a draft. No safety manager, lawyer or OSHA official has checked the answers.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads one document: 29 CFR Part 1904, OSHA's regulation on recording and reporting occupational injuries and illnesses, with its industry lists, as the eCFR showed it on 12 November 2026. We turned the XML into plain text and changed no words. Its one image, the decision tree in 1904.4, is not included, and no task needs it. The eCFR is an editorial compilation, not the official legal edition. This test set is not approved or endorsed by OSHA or the Department of Labor.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the scenarios. They describe no real person, employer or event. Every answer follows from the text and the facts in the question. Each answer was worked out step by step from the line of the regulation it rests on. A second script, sharing no code with the first, recomputed every answer and agreed. Both come from the same reading of the text, so they catch slips of arithmetic, not a different reading of a rule. The regulation gives worked examples only for the electronic submission dates, so the other readings could only be checked against its wording.

## What it does not cover

- US federal rules only, without the state plans, as they stood on 12 November 2026. Part 1904 was last amended in April 2024 and may change.
- Only the regulation's text. OSHA's interpretation letters, its recordkeeping handbook and the instructions on the forms were not used, and they may read it differently.
- Each question states the counting rules the regulation leaves open, such as which days are business days.
- The scenarios are clear-cut on purpose, so the borderline situations that fill much of real recordkeeping are scarce. Questions that need a person's call, such as whether an event significantly aggravated an earlier condition, are not asked.
- Incidence rates, the age correction tables for hearing tests, the four-hour rule for government requests for records and the variance procedure are left out.
- Two to four tasks for each kind of work show where an AI stands. They can't separate two close results.
- It is not legal advice.
