## What this is

A test set on family and medical leave under US federal law. Each of its 30 tasks gives the facts about one unnamed employee and employer, and the AI applies the Department of Labor's FMLA regulations the way a leave administrator would. It tests eligibility and coverage on the right dates, turns intermittent leave into a share of the employee's own workweek, keeps several 12-month periods apart, combines military caregiver leave with other leave, and gives notice and certification deadlines as dates. The tasks fall into ten kinds of work.

This is a draft. No leave administrator, employment lawyer or Department of Labor official has checked the answers.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads one document: 29 CFR Part 825, the regulations under the Family and Medical Leave Act, as the eCFR showed them on 12 November 2026. We turned the XML into plain text and changed no words. We left out subpart G, on other laws, employer practices and collective agreements, which no task uses. The eCFR is an editorial compilation, not the official legal edition. This test set is not approved or endorsed by the Department of Labor.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the scenarios. They describe no real situation. Every answer follows from the regulation, the facts in the question and the counting rules each question states. One script computed the answers with rules that reproduce the regulation's own numbered examples. A second script, sharing no code with the first, recomputed every answer and agreed. Both come from the same reading of the text, so they catch slips of arithmetic, not a different reading of a rule.

## What it does not cover

- US federal rules only, as they stood on 12 November 2026. Part 825 was last amended in January 2025 and may change.
- Only the text of Part 825. The Act itself, court decisions, the Department's opinion letters, state leave laws and collective agreements are left out, and any of them can change a real outcome.
- Part 825 leaves some counting open, such as what a business day is or whether the day of an event counts. Each question states how to count, and a leave administrator may count differently.
- Questions that need a person's call, such as whether a condition is a serious health condition, are given as facts. Key employees, benefits, reinstatement, joint employers and qualifying exigency leave are left out.
- The scenarios are tidy on purpose, with whole weeks and whole days, and are not a sample of real leave requests. Two to four tasks for each kind of work show where an AI stands. They can't separate two close results.
- It is not legal advice.
