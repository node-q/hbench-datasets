## What this is

A test set on the tax amounts the Internal Revenue Service adjusts for inflation each year. Each of its 29 tasks states an unnamed taxpayer's facts, such as a filing status, a taxable income, an age or a gift. The AI reads the amounts for 2025 and 2026 and applies them: the tax from the rate tables, the standard deduction, capital gains rates, the alternative minimum tax exemption, the earned income credit, the kiddie tax, and gift and estate exclusions. The tasks fall into ten kinds of work, such as tax from the rate table, filing status and changes between the two years.

This is a draft. No tax preparer and nobody at the Internal Revenue Service has checked the answers.

## Where the text comes from

The AI reads two IRS revenue procedures as the Internal Revenue Bulletin publishes them. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 (Bulletin 2025-45, 3 November 2025) sets the amounts for tax year 2026 and replaces two of the 2025 items. Rev. Proc. 2024-40 (Bulletin 2024-45, 4 November 2024) set the amounts for tax year 2025. We took both from the Bulletin's web edition on 15 November 2026, when no revenue procedure for 2027 had appeared yet, and turned them into plain text with no words changed. Only the revenue procedures are kept, less the closing section that names their author. The web edition was not compared with the printed one. This test set is not approved, endorsed, sponsored or authorized by, or associated with, the Internal Revenue Service or the Department of the Treasury.

## How the answers were worked out

We wrote the taxpayers, and none is a real person. The revenue procedures give amounts, not the tax law's computations. Where a question needs one, such as the capital gains split, the exemption phaseout or the credit formula, it writes the rule out, cut down to the situation asked. Each answer was worked out step by step from the amounts on the documents' own lines. A second script, sharing no code with the first, worked out every tax bracket by bracket, recomputed every answer and agreed. The rate tables match their own printed figures, and the documents' figures confirm the percentages stated for the minimum tax phaseout and the earned income credit. Tax amounts are accepted within a dollar or 0.1 percent. Amounts read from the documents must be exact.

## What it does not cover

- It is not tax advice. The rules of the Internal Revenue Code that the questions write out are our restatements. The capital gains split, the tentative minimum tax, the kiddie tax, the estate tax and the count of additional standard deduction amounts have no worked figure in the documents to confirm them.
- The tax from the rate table can differ by a few dollars from the tax tables and credit tables in the form instructions, which are not in the documents. Worksheets are left out.
- Some amounts are read but never computed with, such as those of the qualified business income deduction, the child tax credit and the adoption credit's phaseouts. Business, bond and penalty items are left out.
- US federal tax only, for tax years 2025 and 2026. Later laws or guidance may have changed these amounts, and we did not check.
